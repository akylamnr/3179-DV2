Australia's Flowers and Horticulture

From farm gates to global markets, explore how production, international trade and consumer demand shape Australia's horticulture and specifically flower industry.

1. An Overview of Horticulture

Horticulture is a branch of agriculture that focuses on the growth of fruits, vegetables, nuts, wine grapes and flower-related groups.

Each square in the chart represents approximately 1% of the combined value of the five categories of horticulture in Australia.

17.3% of the combined farm-gate value is made up of flowers (2024-25).

Source: Australian Buraeu of Statistics - ABS, Table 1: Horticulture summary estimates by Australia, state and territory (2024-25).

Two ways to measure growth

Nuts grew fastest proportionally, rising 38.5% from A$1.20 billion to A$1.66 billion. Fruit grew by 25.3%, but added A$1.48 billion in value - much more than nuts.

Meanwhile, the flower category barely changed.
Its combined farm-gate value rose by just 1.4% between 2020–21 and 2024–25.

These are nominal values, not adjusted for inflation, and measure farm-gate value rather than physical production. Farm-gate value refers to the value of crops at the farm level.

4. Why does Australia import flowers?

Each bar is one of the six leading origin countries, stacked by flower type. Kenya and Ecuador have large rose segments, while Malaysia and Vietnam have large chrysanthemum segments. Lilies are omitted because their import value is very small.

Source: UN Comtrade, Australia imports by partner and product, calendar 2024, HS 060311-060319, H6 classification. Values are in US dollars. The six origins are the largest by total value across cut-flower categories. Lilies are excluded. Partner records carry isReported=false in the Comtrade response, so origin-level values should be treated as indicative and the chart does not show all Australian imports.

Source: UN Comtrade, HS 0603, 2024. Unit value is import value divided by reported weight.

What does the price tell us?

Dividing import value by reported weight estimates the average trade value per kilogram for each supplier. This Comtrade measure is in US dollars for calendar year 2024, so it should not be compared directly with the Handbook's Australian-dollar series.

The story is not simply local versus imported. Australian growers supply a substantial domestic industry, imports help meet demand, and selected native flowers reach overseas markets. Different datasets describe different points in that chain, which is why the financial-year Handbook figures and calendar-year Comtrade comparisons are kept distinct.

06 - The workforce behind the harvest

Production also depends on people. ABARES survey estimates show how horticulture farms staff peak seasons and how that differs by state.

Nationally, the share of farms using overseas workers in peak months rose from 25.7% to 40.4% between 2021–22 and 2023–24, a rise of 14.7 percentage points. Every state increased. Northern Territory and Tasmania are highest; their estimates are less precise (higher RSE) because they are based on fewer farms.

Source: ABARES, Labour use in Australian horticulture: Analysis of survey results, 2023–24, Table 2 (CC BY 4.0). Values are survey estimates; RSE is the relative standard error.

Use the menu to switch measures. Bars show how far each state sits above (pink) or below (purple) the Australian figure; the deeper the colour, the bigger the gap; the number at the end of each bar is the state's own percentage. For overseas workers in peak months, the Northern Territory, Tasmania and Western Australia are furthest above the national 40.4%, while South Australia and New South Wales are below. The Northern Territory has high RSE on most measures, so read it with caution.

Source: ABARES, Labour use in Australian horticulture, 2023–24, Table 2. Overseas workers (peak months), hard to recruit (“lots of difficulty”), accommodation provided and machinery use are shares of farms; piece rates is a share of workers; labour costs is a share of operating expenditure.

Peak-month labour use is concentrated in a few regions: Victoria's North West (14,100 workers), Melbourne and Mornington Peninsula (11,360) and South Australia's South East (10,220) lead. Circles sit at an area-weighted centre of each region, not at individual farms. Several estimates are imprecise: Perth - West has an RSE of 64% and Mid North Coast, New England and North West 44%.

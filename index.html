Australia's flower industry

Grown here, flown in

Australia grows some of the world's most distinctive flowers. So why are so many of the flowers we buy grown overseas?

Follow the flowers from Australian farms to the supermarket bucket: how big the industry is, where it grows, where imports come from, and what those imports cost.

01 · The big picture

Flowers are one slice of a much bigger industry

Before looking at imports, it helps to understand the scale of local horticulture. Horticulture spans crops grown on farms and in nurseries, including fruit, vegetables, nuts, wine grapes, flowers, nursery plants and turf.

What stands out: Cut flowers, nurseries and turf account for about 17.3% of the combined value of the five main horticulture groups shown in 2024-25. Fruit and vegetables contribute much larger shares.

A note on scope: The ABS groups cut flowers, nurseries and turf together, so this is not a flower-only estimate.

02 · Growing flowers at home

Local production is concentrated - and barely growing

Australian flower growing is concentrated in a few places. The state map shows where reported cut-flower value is highest; the trend chart then puts the wider flower-related group in context alongside other horticulture categories.

What stands out: Victoria leads the state map, with reported cut-flower value of about A$114.4 million. Cut flowers are grown year-round, with Victoria the leading producing state. Australia's Department of Agriculture also describes the sector's year-round production and seasonal peaks.

What stands out: The combined value of flowers, nurseries and turf rose by just 1.4% from 2020-21 to 2024-25. Over the same period, nuts grew by 38.5%, fruit by about 25.3% and vegetables by about 22.9%, while wine grapes fell by 27.5%.

If local flower-related value is barely changing, who supplies the flowers sold here? Flower Industry Australia estimates that about half of the flowers sold in Australia are imported.

03 · Flowers on the move

Most imported flowers come from a handful of countries

Imported flowers arrive from several continents, but the trade is concentrated. In the 2024 data shown here, six supplier countries account for about 91% of the reported value of Australian imports of fresh-cut flowers and buds.

What stands out: Malaysia, Vietnam and China are in Asia; Kenya is in Africa; Ecuador and Colombia are in South America. The arcs show that import value is not evenly spread across suppliers.

What stands out: Malaysia's reported import value peaked at about US$24.0 million in 2022. Vietnam's value then rose from about US$6.2 million in 2023 to US$11.5 million in 2024. Darker cells indicate higher reported import value.

The heatmap shows how much was imported; the bump chart shows who ranked first. Kenya led in the earlier years, but Malaysia moved ahead in 2021 and remained the largest supplier among these six countries through 2024.

04 · The big question

Price helps explain why imports compete

Geography tells us where the flowers come from; import unit values give another clue to how they compete in the market. Dividing trade value by weight provides a rough value per kilogram.

Across the suppliers shown, import unit values are roughly US$7-US$9 per kilogram. These are trade unit values, not supermarket prices. The chart does not include a comparable Australian-grown price, so it cannot prove the price gap by itself. Flower Industry Australia points to lower labour and production costs overseas as one reason imports can compete on price.

Peak occasions such as Valentine's Day and Mother's Day also create sharp demand, while Australian production has seasonal patterns of its own. At the same time, supermarkets are not generally required to display country-of-origin labels for cut flowers, which can make it difficult for shoppers to tell where a bunch was grown.

The takeaway

So why do so many flowers fly in?

The evidence points to three parts of the paradox:

  • Local production is concentrated and the wider flower-related group is barely growing. Its combined farm-gate value rose only 1.4% in four years, far below growth in nuts, fruit and vegetables.
  • A handful of overseas suppliers fill a large share of the market. Six countries account for about 91% of the reported value in the 2024 fresh-cut flower trade data, and Malaysia overtook Kenya in the selected supplier rankings.
  • Price, peak demand and limited origin information shape the choice. Overseas suppliers compete on production costs, demand rises around major gifting days, and origin is often not clearly labelled at the point of sale.

Australia can grow flowers the world wants, but imports remain a major part of the bouquets we buy, and shoppers may not always know where the stems were grown.

What you can do: Ask your florist or supermarket where the flowers were grown, and look for Australian-grown labels.

About this data

The ABS groups cut flowers, nurseries and turf together in the horticulture charts, so those charts are not flower-only estimates. The state map, sector trend chart and international trade charts also use different measures and periods.

Horticulture farm-gate values are in Australian dollars; trade values are in US dollars. They cover different scopes and should not be compared as if they were the same measure. The import unit value is calculated from reported import value divided by weight and reflects each supplier's mix of flowers. It is not a retail price. Farm-gate values are nominal and have not been adjusted for inflation.

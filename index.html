Australia's Flowers and Horticulture

Grown here, flown in: A closer look at Australia's flower and horticulture industry.

Australia grows some of the world's most distinctive flowers. So why are so many of the flowers we buy grown overseas? Follow the flowers from Australian farms to the supermarket bucket: how big the industry is, where it grows, where imports come from, and what they cost.

Flowers in the bigger picture

Before looking at imports, let's examine the place of flowers and related industries within Australian horticulture.

02 - WHERE FLOWERS GROW

Growing flowers at home

Australian cut-flower production is concentrated in particular states. The map shows where production is worth the most, while the line chart reveals how the wider flower, nursery and turf industry has changed over time.

Victoria stands out as the leading state for cut-flower production, with a reported value of A$114.4 million. The map shows the value produced in one year, rather than how production has changed over time.

Data: ABS Australian Agriculture: Horticulture, 2024-25. Boundaries: ABS State and Territory Boundaries.

The flower-related category barely changed. Its combined farm-gate value rose by 1.4% between 2020-21 and 2024-25, while nuts grew by 38.5% and wine grapes declined by 27.5%.

These are nominal values, not adjusted for inflation, and measure farm-gate value rather than physical production.

03 - FLOWERS ON THE MOVE

Flowers that travel the world

Australia's local flower industry is concentrated in particular states, but the flowers sold here also come from overseas. Six countries supplied about 91% of the reported value of Australia's fresh-cut flower imports in 2024. Where do they come from, and how have these suppliers changed over time?

Which countries supply the most flowers?

Australia's flower imports connect suppliers across three regions: Malaysia, Vietnam and China in Asia, Kenya in Africa, and Ecuador and Colombia in South America. The flow map shows the value moving from these suppliers to Australia.

Source: International merchandise trade statistics.

Source: UN Comtrade, annual flower-import values for the years shown in the chart.

04 - THE BIG QUESTION

So why do so many flowers fly in?

The evidence points to a combination of local industry trends, established overseas suppliers and price. Imported flowers arrive from a relatively small group of countries, while the value of Australia's wider flower-related horticulture category has barely changed. Import values also give us a way to compare the average value of flowers arriving from different suppliers.

Source: UN Comtrade, HS 0603, 2024. Unit value is import value divided by reported weight.

What does the price tell us?

Dividing import value by the weight of flowers imported gives an estimate of the average trade value per kilogram for each supplier.

Why it matters: Differences in value per kilogram may reflect the types and mix of flowers supplied, as well as differences in price.

These figures are trade values, not supermarket prices. Since this chart does not show a comparable price for Australian-grown flowers, it cannot prove the size of the price gap between local and imported flowers.

What shoppers can do

Ask your florist or supermarket where the stems were grown, and look for Australian-grown labels when choosing flowers.
